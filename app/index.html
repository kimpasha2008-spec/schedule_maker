Year 1 Section Planner

Every class keeps its published day, time and room. The planner only decides which lecture, tutorial and lab section each student joins, so that everyone gets few gaps, no clashes, class hours spread evenly from Monday to Friday, and no building gets an overcrowded hour. It is compared on the same simulated students with three ways of registering: a random clash-free timetable, today's first come, first served, and first come, first served where a student drops a course rather than accept a clash.

How the numbers were made

Class times, rooms and seat caps come from the published Semester A 2026/27 timetable (1,585 courses, 5,056 sections). The students are simulated: each major's Year 1 study plan and intake estimate, scaled so every course fits its real seats. GE1601 is online and Gateway electives are not modelled.

Random clash-free and first come, first served both let students register one at a time in random order. In the random version each student gets a random timetable with no clashes from the seats still open; in first come, first served each picks their own best one. When no clash-free choice is left, first come, first served accepts the smallest clash, and the no-clash version drops as few courses as needed instead. The planner assigns everyone together, in groups of up to 12 students from the same major. It is an OR-Tools CP-SAT model solved by large-neighbourhood search: it repeatedly frees a few groups, keeps everyone else fixed, and re-solves that piece, keeping only improvements. It weighs the goals listed above, and the first-years can only use the seats left after other years' share is held back (other years keep what they really enrolled, as long as first-years still fit within 95% of the seats).

All figures are counted for every teaching week (weeks 1 to 13) and averaged. Daily-hours spread is the standard deviation of hours on campus across the days a student comes in.
