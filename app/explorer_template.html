Assumptions behind these numbers
  • Population. 183 real Semester A timetables (from students' screenshots) were bootstrapped to about 14,600 undergraduates: each extra student copies the shape of a random real student's week, swapping courses within the same subject and level in proportion to the AIMS seats each course still had to fill. Three such populations were built; figures average them.
  • Programme. Students carry no major, so the programme is the subject they take most, ignoring GE, PE, language and service maths/science courses. Business common-core (CB) courses mark most Year 1 and 2 business students. Programmes under 150 students are grouped as Other.
  • Today (random picks). Each student holds a random clash-free mix of open sections. The real screenshots average more gap hours than this, so it understates today's problem.
  • Planner. Re-assigns sections for everyone, worst timetables first, with seat swaps. One idle hour costs as much as three hours of uneven week; every course is kept; busy buildings are avoided. Class times and seat caps never change.
  • Intervals. 95% bootstrap intervals resample students within a programme against today's random picks.
