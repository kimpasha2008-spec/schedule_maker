How the numbers were made
  • Real timetables. 199 screenshots shared by students were read by hand; 189 were Semester A 2026/27 and 183 had undergraduate courses with fixed times. Only course and section codes were kept.
  • Scaling to all of CityU (bootstrap). The real students stay as they are. More students are made by drawing a real student at random and copying the shape of their week: each of their courses is kept or swapped for another course of the same subject and level, weighted by how many AIMS seats that course still has to fill. A course is only added where a seat that fits the student's week is left. Students are added until the AIMS undergraduate enrolment of all 740 timed courses is used up. The population count and coverage are in Sources.
  • Random clash-free (today). Each student holds a random clash-free mix of open sections. The real screenshots look like this or worse, which makes it a cautious stand-in for today.
  • First come, best pick. Students register in random order and each takes the best timetable still open. It is the best case for today's system, and late students can miss a course.
  • Planner. Starting from today, sections are re-assigned for everyone, worst timetables first, with seat swaps between students. It scores one idle hour like three hours of uneven week, keeps every course, and avoids each building's busiest hours. Class times and seat caps never change.
  • Metrics. Gap hours: idle hours between a student's first and last class each day. Uneven week: average distance of a weekday's hours from an even fifth of the week. Building peaks: sum over buildings of the busiest hour's students in class.
  • Limits. Courses without fixed times, postgraduate-only courses and restrictions such as major-only sections are left out. The sample over-represents first-years; the demand matching corrects course mix but not personal preferences.
